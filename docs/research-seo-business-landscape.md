# Selling SEO after AI ate the click

A new SEO and ads business can still win in late 2026, but not as a classic agency selling hours of audits, keyword lists, blog posts and backlinks. Two things changed the market. AI answers now sit on roughly **43–48% of Google queries** and cut the top organic result's click-through rate by about **58%** where they appear. Self-serve AI SEO tools at **$49–$249 a month** now undercut the most common SMB retainer bracket of **$501–$1,000 a month**. Demand has not collapsed, though. **84% of agencies** still rank SEO among their most promising channels, **66% report client demand for AI-search optimization (AEO/GEO)**, and spend is shifting toward measurable visibility and paid search. The best-supported model for a founder with software skills is a **hybrid**. It has a software-automated core (audits, keyword and competitor research, reporting, AI-visibility tracking), sold as productized packages with published prices to one chosen vertical. Human-delivered premium add-ons sit on top: expert-reviewed content, digital PR, implementation and Google Ads management. Of the five services the founder listed, audits and keyword research are easy to build but commoditized. Content and local SEO are feasible with human review and some gated API access. Link building, as usually sold, is the highest policy and reputation risk and should be rebuilt as earned links and digital PR. Five services are missing from the list and should be added: AI-search visibility, technical implementation, analytics and reporting, review management, and paid search. The operating rules that prevent the industry's worst failures belong in the product from day one. Clients own every account. The firm works as a named, least-privilege user. Every site change is logged and reversible. Nothing is ever promised as a ranking guarantee.

## AI answers halved the click, yet SEO spend is reallocating rather than collapsing

Nobody agrees how big the market is, and a founder should not build a pitch deck on any single figure. Mordor Intelligence sizes global fee-based SEO services at **$83.98B in 2026**, growing at **11.78% a year to $164.3B by 2032**, with North America holding **33.68%** of spend ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/seo-market?no_head=1)). The same page cites two other estimates of the 2025 market, **$85.7B and $2.6B**, a roughly 33-fold spread that Mordor blames on coverage and modeling choices ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/seo-market?no_head=1)). "SEO software" estimates such as Precedence Research's **$96.42B for 2026** ([Precedence Research](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/seo-software-market)) fail a basic sanity check. The category leader, Semrush, sold to Adobe for about **$1.9B in cash** in a deal that closed on April 28, 2026 ([VectorShift](https://vectorshift.ai/research/deals/adobe-semrush-2026)). That price is implausible if annual software revenue in the category were really near $100B. A defensible framing is "tens of billions globally, roughly $25–30B in the US, growing at about 10–13% a year." The US figure is our inference from the North American share.

The structural shift matters more than the market size. Similarweb estimates AI Overviews appear on about 43% of Google queries and Semrush puts it at about 48%, up from roughly 13–20% a year earlier ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/ai-overviews-half-of-google-queries/)). Ahrefs' study of about 300,000 keywords found **position-one CTR falls 58% on average when an AI Overview is present** ([Shopifreaks on Ahrefs](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-ai-overviews-now-reduce-top-ranking-pages-clickthrough-rates-by-58-according-to-new-ahrefs-research-analyzing-300000-keywords/)). SparkToro's clickstream panel found **68.01% of US Google searches ended without a click** in January–April 2026, up from 60.45% in 2024 ([TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/reports-data/google-zero-click-searches-68-percent-2026)). Google says AI Mode passed **1 billion monthly users**, yet the same panel shows only **0.34% of US Google searches** moved into AI Mode ([TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/reports-data/google-zero-click-searches-68-percent-2026)). The two numbers almost certainly measure different things: exposure versus active use. Neither should anchor a forecast.

For positioning, the key detail is that the disruption depends heavily on search intent. Seer Interactive's 2026 data shows AI Overviews on about **95% of comparison queries, 86% of question queries and 36% of informational queries, but only about 5% of transactional queries** ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ctr-recovery-study-475566)). Seer also found that organic CTR on AI-Overview queries is about **2.1% when the brand is cited in the overview versus 0.9% when it is not**, against 3.3% where no overview appears ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ctr-recovery-study-475566)). This has two consequences. A business selling informational blog traffic is selling the asset AI erodes fastest, while commercial and local-intent SEO is comparatively protected. "AI visibility" is also no longer a vanity metric: being cited more than doubles the clicks a brand keeps. Outside Google, the chatbot market is fragmenting. ChatGPT's share of generative-AI web traffic fell from about 76% to 53% between June 2025 and May 2026, while Gemini rose to about 27–28% and Claude to about 9% ([Similarweb](https://aisearch.similarweb.com/blog/gen-ai-stats/)). Tracking has to cover several engines; a "ChatGPT SEO" offer alone is too narrow.

Budgets are bending, not breaking. In Search Engine Journal's survey, **43% of companies cut some SEO spending** in the prior year, but **65% expect no cuts** in the next ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/the-state-of-seo-2026-how-to-survive/555368/)). AgencyAnalytics' 2026 survey of 494 agency professionals found **84% still see SEO as one of the most promising channels**. **66% report client demand for AEO/AI-search SEO**, a category that did not exist in earlier editions. **41% report new difficulty measuring ROI**, and **40% are moving money into paid search** ([AgencyAnalytics](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/agency-benchmarks-2026-ai-search)). Capital is following the measurement problem, not the agency model. About **$619M** has been disclosed across ten GEO/AEO startups. Profound alone raised a **$180M Series D at a $1.8B valuation** in September 2026, and Sitecore bought Scrunch for about **$225M** in June 2026 ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/ai-search-optimization-startups-2026-geo-aeo-funding-investors)). Strategic buyers and venture investors are paying for AI-visibility software aimed at enterprises. That leaves the job of turning this data into outcomes for smaller businesses to service firms. A founder does not need to out-build Profound. The founder can rent the same data through APIs and sell what it means.

## Four openings sit between $99 tools and $15,000 enterprise retainers

The 2026 price ladder has a visible hole in the middle. At the bottom, autonomous tools such as Search Atlas OTTO start at **$99 a month**, Alli AI at **$249**, and Surfer and Frase at **$49** ([AI Growth Agent comparison](https://aigrowthagent.co/articles/best-autonomous-seo-platforms/)). That comparison is vendor-authored, and Alli AI's price appears elsewhere as anywhere from $89 to $1,249 a month ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/reviews/alli-ai)). Peec AI's AI-search analytics starts at **€75 a month** ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/ai-search-optimization-startups-2026-geo-aeo-funding-investors)). Just above those tools sits the bulk of human SEO. The most-cited pricing survey found the modal retainer bracket was **$501–$1,000 a month** and **68.8% of providers charge $2,000 or less** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing)). That data dates from about 2023, and no comparable 2025–26 survey exists. Dedicated AEO retainers start at **$1,000–$3,500** for basic schema and Google-only monitoring, rise to **$4,000–$12,000** for multi-engine coverage, and reach **$15,000–$50,000+** at enterprise level ([310 Creative](https://www.310creative.com/blog/aeo-agency-pricing)). At the top, Profound already serves **700+ enterprise customers, about 10% of the Fortune 500** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/24/exclusive-as-ai-threatens-search-profound-raises-96-million-to-help-brands-stay-visible)). Enterprise AI visibility is well served. Small and mid-sized businesses are not.

The first opening is **affordable AI-visibility measurement and optimization for SMBs, local businesses and multi-location brands**. Most of the work overlaps with good SEO: structured data, answer-first content, original statistics, third-party mentions and reviews. That keeps delivery cost low for a software-assisted team. The same overlap makes the service hard to differentiate on technique alone, so the edge has to come from price, packaging and vertical focus. The second opening is **transparency as a product feature**. A survey of 500+ businesses by Artios found that **secrecy and lack of transparency caused 25% of SEO-supplier terminations**, the leading cause in the first six months. Missed long-term targets caused 29% ([DecisionMarketing](https://www.decisionmarketing.co.uk/news/agencies-pay-high-price-for-seo-smoke-and-mirrors)). The survey's publication date is uncertain and may be 2016. More recent sources point the same way: **70% of agencies say client reporting is critical to retention** ([AgencyAnalytics 2025](https://agencyanalytics.com/company/newsroom/2025-marketing-agency-benchmarks-report)), and SEOmonitor names unrealistic expectations as the top churn cause ([SEOmonitor](https://www.seomonitor.com/learning-hub/the-complete-strategy-guide-to-reducing-churn-increasing-client-retention-for-seo-agencies)). Agencies are also handing out AI reporting "at no additional charge" ([AgencyAnalytics](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/agency-benchmarks-2026-ai-search)), which means an unpriced deliverable is waiting to be packaged.

The third opening is **unified organic and paid search**, which fits the founder's interest in running ads. Seer found paid CTR on AI-Overview queries rose from **14.6% to 16.2%** while paid CTR on other queries fell from **26% to 21.8%** ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ctr-recovery-study-475566)). Brands cited in AI Overviews also earn higher paid CTR, **15.74% versus 11.19%** ([TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/reports-data/google-zero-click-searches-68-percent-2026)). Organic citations and paid clicks now reinforce each other, so a firm managing both can make a combined-attribution case that a pure SEO shop cannot. The fourth opening is **implementation that the client keeps**. Agencies charge **$5,000–$15,000** for AEO implementation projects ([310 Creative](https://www.310creative.com/blog/aeo-agency-pricing)). The cheap alternative, JavaScript overlays, has a known flaw: changes "revert to their original state when you remove the script" ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/reviews/alli-ai)). A firm that ships fixes into the client's own CMS or codebase, with a change log, gives clients durable work they can audit.

These gaps should be treated as hypotheses, not proven segments. The research found **no quantitative data on SMB willingness to pay** for AI visibility, nor on how often agencies fail to implement what they recommend. The gaps could also narrow quickly. Search Atlas already markets a white-label "Agency Hub" and an "AI marketing department" ([Search Atlas](https://searchatlas.com/lp/ai-marketing-department/)), and Adobe now owns Semrush's distribution. The window is real but probably measured in a few years, not a decade.

## Two founder services are easy, two are medium, and link building needs redesign

The services split cleanly by whether they *read data* or *act on the world*. Services that read data (crawling, Search Console analysis, SERP and keyword data, rank tracking) are 80–95% automatable with today's APIs and LLMs. Services that publish content at scale or acquire links run into Google's spam policies. Services built on relationships or high-stakes judgment, such as digital PR, migrations and penalty recovery, stay human. The ratings below are a synthesis of the research for a two-to-five-person team that writes its own software.

**SEO audits** are the easiest to build and the hardest to charge much for. A credible audit engine combines a crawler, PageSpeed and CrUX data, and the free Search Console API. That API allows **1,200 Search Analytics queries per minute per site**, but URL Inspection is capped at **2,000 queries per day per site** ([Google Search Console API limits](https://developers.google.cn/webmaster-tools/limits?hl=it)). Index monitoring of large e-commerce sites therefore has to work by sampling, not full census. The catch is commoditization. Per-project SEO fees cluster at **$2,501–$5,000** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing)), and Clutch shows technical and on-page work listed as low as **$25–49 an hour** offshore ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/seo-firms/pricing)). Google's own hiring advice says a good SEO should deliver a *prioritized* audit with estimated investment and impact ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-advice-on-how-to-hire-an-seo/351710/)). Prioritization, grounded in real crawl data rather than LLM guesswork, is where the value lies. The audit works best as an entry product and a lead magnet, not a profit center.

**Keyword research** is similarly easy and similarly commoditized. DataForSEO sells Google SERP data from **$0.0006 per SERP** (about $0.60 per thousand in standard queue) alongside keyword, backlink, Labs and AI-optimization endpoints ([DataForSEO](https://dataforseo.com/pricing/serp/serp-api); [diyai.io](https://diyai.io/ai-tools/seo/seo-data-apis/)). LLMs handle clustering and intent labeling well. Volume figures are vendor estimates that disagree with each other, so the human contribution is choosing which clusters matter to the business. In 2026 that means weighting transactional and local intent, which AI Overviews touch far less.

**Content creation and optimization** is feasible but must be built around human review. Google does not ban AI content. Its March 2024 scaled-content-abuse policy applies "whether automation, humans or a combination are involved," and Google reported a **45% reduction** in low-quality, unoriginal content after that update ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/)). The quality rater guidelines now treat unedited AI output produced at scale as a "Lowest" quality example ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-updates-search-quality-rater-guidelines/2026/10/)). One trade source reports that Google added guidance on **October 1, 2026** that AI-generated content should be manually fact-checked before publication ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/)). Confirm this against Google's own documentation before quoting it to clients. The workable model is AI-drafted briefs, refreshes and first drafts, with expert input and a logged fact-check step. Volume is never the product. SEJ's survey found **original content creation (66%) and content updates (42.6%)** are the activities SEOs credit with the most positive impact ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/the-state-of-seo-2026-how-to-survive/555368/)), which supports selling refreshes and original research over article counts.

**Local SEO** is medium difficulty, mainly because of gated access, and it is the most strategically attractive of the five. Google Business Profile APIs "aren't open to the public." Access requires an application with a legitimate business reason, ideally a domain email and a live website, and **review takes up to 14 days**. Even after approval, the firm still needs access to each individual profile ([GBP API FAQ](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/faq)). Citation distribution is already fully productized. BrightLocal pushes listings to aggregators such as Data Axle and Foursquare at **$2–$3.20 per citation** plus **$120 a year** for aggregator renewals ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/local-data-aggregators)), so reselling beats building. Local retainers averaged **$1,557 a month** in the Ahrefs survey ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing)). Local searches are mostly transactional, so they sit in the part of Google that AI Overviews disrupt least. The main risks are profile suspensions, doorway-style location pages and review tactics that violate FTC rules.

**Link building, as the market usually sells it, is hard and dangerous.** Only prospecting, reclamation of lost links and unlinked-mention discovery automate well, which is roughly 40% of the work. Google's June 2026 spam update targeted link schemes and low-quality networks, and once SpamBrain neutralizes those links, "the artificial benefit is permanently lost" ([GEL Studios](https://www.gelstudios.co.uk/insights/may-june-google-core-update-26/)). Market prices show why the temptation persists. BuzzStream's October 2026 marketplace data puts the average link insertion at **$179**, a guest post at **$295 bought direct or $461 through a vendor** (a markup of about 56%), and **$2,983** for a guest post on a DR80+ site. Only **1.37% of guest-post opportunities met BuzzStream's quality bar** ([BuzzStream](https://www.buzzstream.com/?p=8476)). Reselling paid placements earns a good margin, but it puts the firm's name on exactly the tactics Google is neutralizing and invites site-reputation-abuse problems. Rebuild this service as **earned links**: digital PR campaigns built on original data (about **$1,250–$1,500 per link** or **$5,000–$10,000 per campaign** at market rates, per [BuzzStream](https://www.buzzstream.com/?p=8476)), plus link reclamation and unlinked-mention conversion. All of it should be reported transparently, link by link.

### Five missing services and the full catalog

The founder's list reads like a 2019 menu. It leaves out **AI-search visibility (GEO/AEO)**, the fastest-growing demand category, and **paid search management**, the service **89% of agencies** now offer and the one **68%** expect to deliver the strongest results ([AgencyAnalytics 2025](https://agencyanalytics.com/company/newsroom/2025-marketing-agency-benchmarks-report)). AI visibility is now buildable as software because the data vendors sell it. DataForSEO offers LLM-mention and AI-keyword APIs, Ahrefs Brand Radar covers AI responses, SerpApi has AI Overview and AI Mode endpoints, and SE Ranking has an AI Search API ([diyai.io](https://diyai.io/ai-tools/seo/seo-data-apis/)). Tracking is easy. Optimization is not. The academic basis is a single 2023 preprint showing **visibility lifts of up to 40%** from adding citations, quotations and statistics ([Everything-PR summary](https://everything-pr.com/we-found-the-paper-that-launched-geo-heres-whats-actually-in-it/)). Profound's own data shows **up to 90% of cited sources change over time** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/24/exclusive-as-ai-threatens-search-profound-raises-96-million-to-help-brands-stay-visible)). GEO should be sold as measurement plus disciplined content and PR work, never as guaranteed placement.

Paid search management fits the market because clients are moving money there and many accounts are badly run. WordStream's analysis found about **29% of Google Ads accounts recorded zero conversions in 90 days**, while average CPC rose to **$5.42** and cost per lead to **$70.11** in 2025 ([Shopifreaks on WordStream/LocaliQ](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-ads-cpcs-rose-to-5-42-in-2025-with-87-of-industries-seeing-increases-but-conversion-rates-climbed-to-8-18-as-automation-improves/)). Agencies report that **55% of clients ask for performance-based paid ads** ([AgencyAnalytics](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/agency-benchmarks-2026-ai-search)). Paid search also produces attribution data within weeks while SEO takes months, which helps retention during the early-churn windows. This research did *not* cover ads fee models (percentage of spend versus flat fee), the Google Ads API or Partner-program requirements. Those need their own pass before the functional design.

## Build a delivery platform and a free grader; rent the commodity data

What the founder must build follows from the services. The firm does not need its own crawler index, keyword database or LLM-monitoring infrastructure; vendors sell all of that by usage. Ahrefs includes API units from its Lite plan (**100,000 units a month**). Semrush requires a Business subscription plus separately purchased units that expire. SerpApi starts at **$25 for 1,000 searches**, and Bright Data at **$1.50 per 1,000** requests ([diyai.io](https://diyai.io/ai-tools/seo/seo-data-apis/)). Most of these vendors, including DataForSEO, Ahrefs, Semrush and SE Ranking, now ship official **MCP servers**, so an LLM agent can query SERP, keyword and backlink data directly ([diyai.io](https://diyai.io/ai-tools/seo/seo-data-apis/)). The cheapest stack combines the free Google first-party APIs, pay-per-use DataForSEO for SERP, keyword, backlink and LLM-mention data, an off-the-shelf crawler, and a white-label citation layer such as BrightLocal. Data costs then scale with client count instead of being a fixed seat cost.

The proprietary layer is the *operating system* around that data. Each component maps to a failure mode found in the research. The firm needs an **intake and proposal flow** that captures Google's recommended discovery questions and sets expectations, because unrealistic expectations drive early churn. It needs an **access-provisioning tracker** that sends role-specific invite instructions per platform and records what was granted, by whom and when. That record is also the offboarding checklist. An **audit and analysis engine** grounded in crawl, Search Console and PageSpeed data, with LLMs used for prioritization and narrative rather than diagnosis, avoids the known failure of LLM misdiagnosis without real data. A **multi-engine AI-visibility tracker** should cover Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity, given the fragmentation Similarweb documents. A **content workflow with enforced human-review and fact-check gates** and an AI-use log addresses Google's scaled-content enforcement. A **change-management layer** records every site change before and after, takes client approval for high-impact edits, runs pre- and post-deployment crawl diffs and keeps a rollback path. A **client portal** reports leads, citations and visibility alongside traffic. Finally, a **public, niche-specific grader** serves as the lead magnet.

One choice shapes the technical design more than any other: does the firm only recommend changes, or does it implement them? Implementation is a differentiator, but it requires write access to client systems. That pulls in the access, security and liability questions covered below. A middle path is to implement through white-label platforms such as Search Atlas OTTO or Alli AI, which deploy through a pixel, plugin or JavaScript snippet ([Search Atlas Help Center](https://help.searchatlas.com/en/articles/11823178-search-atlas-master-faq); [Dupple](https://dupple.com/reviews/alli-ai)). This is faster to launch but inherits their lock-in and rendering trade-offs. The research supports building native CMS and Git-based deployment for the two or three platforms common in the chosen vertical, and treating overlays as a stopgap with exportable change history.

## A hybrid of productized offers, a retainer core and software leverage beats pure agency or pure SaaS

The four candidate models each solve a different problem, and the evidence favors combining them rather than picking one.

| Model | Evidence for | Evidence against | Fit for this founder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic agency (retainers, hours) | 78.2% of SEO providers and 90% of digital agencies use retainers ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing); [Predictable Profits](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)) | Net margins of 18–22% at 7-figure scale; revenue capped by headcount (about $150K per employee) | Viable but wastes the founder's software edge |
| Productized packages | Easy to buy and compare; link and local tiers already sell this way ([BuzzStream](https://www.buzzstream.com/?p=8476)) | Commoditized and price-compared against $99 tools | Good front end; weak alone |
| SaaS | Strategic exits (Semrush, Scrunch) and venture appetite | Competes with Adobe-Semrush, Profound ($1.8B valuation, 1,000+ enterprise customers) and €75 self-serve tools ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/ai-search-optimization-startups-2026-geo-aeo-funding-investors)) | Poor primary model for a bootstrapped entrant |
| Performance-based | 55% of clients ask for it in paid ads ([AgencyAnalytics](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/agency-benchmarks-2026-ai-search)) | 41% of agencies struggle to measure SEO ROI; AI answers break click attribution | Use only for paid ads or controllable outcomes |
| **Hybrid** | Combines retainer predictability, productized sales ease and software margins | More to build and explain | **Recommended** |

The unit economics explain why software leverage matters. Predictable Profits' benchmark of 300+ digital agencies shows 7-figure agencies earning **18–22% net margins** with **78% annual client retention** and a client lifetime value of **$125,000 over 18 months**. 8-figure agencies earn **25–32%** with **92% retention** and **$450,000 over 36 months**. Revenue per employee is **$150,000 versus $225,000** ([Predictable Profits](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)). These are general digital-agency figures, the methodology is undisclosed, and no SEO-specific margin benchmark was found. At the Ahrefs average agency retainer of about **$3,209 a month** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing)), each employee needs about four to six clients to hit those revenue-per-head figures. Automation is how a small firm raises that ratio without lowering quality. The hybrid uses software as internal leverage and as a client-facing transparency layer, not as a product competing for self-serve buyers. A self-serve tier can come later if the grader shows demand.

| One-off SEO + AI-visibility audit with prioritized roadmap | $1,500–$5,000 | AI visibility audits $1,500–$7,500 ([310 Creative](https://www.310creative.com/blog/aeo-agency-pricing)); per-project modal $2,501–$5,000 ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-pricing)) |

| Paid search management | To be determined | Fee models not researched |

Two pricing habits separate healthy agencies from struggling ones. Top performers reassess fees every quarter ([Predictable Profits](https://predictableprofits.com/2025-agency-growth-benchmark-key-metrics-from-300-7-8-figure-agencies/)). And although **64% of agencies plan to raise fees, only 16% raise them for existing clients every year** ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/)). Writing a capped annual escalator into every contract fixes this at no cost.

## Onboard through three gates and write ownership into the contract

Intake works best as three gates, each with a different job. The first is **pre-sale discovery**, which qualifies the client and sets expectations. Google's own advice says a good SEO asks what makes the business unique, who the ideal customer is, how customers find the site today, how the business makes money, what other channels it uses and who its competitors are. Google also says goals, metrics and tracking should be agreed before work starts ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-advice-on-how-to-hire-an-seo/351710/)). A structured form plus a short call should capture the value proposition, goals, budget, personas, competitors and past SEO work ([Surfer](https://surferseo.com/blog/onboarding-seo-clients)). The firm should then score fit and expectations before quoting. Unrealistic expectations are the most-cited churn cause, and churn spikes around **months 6, 12 and 24** ([SEOmonitor](https://www.seomonitor.com/learning-hub/the-complete-strategy-guide-to-reducing-churn-increasing-client-retention-for-seo-agencies)). The cheapest place to stop that churn is before signature.

The second gate is **proposal and contract**. The research supports a clear default template. Market norms are a **3–6 month minimum** (12 months for larger retainers) with **30–60 days' notice**. A 30-day notice combined with annual auto-renewal is a trap that lets the client exit only once a year ([Topify](https://topify.ai/blog/seo-agency-contract-terms-explained)). A 3-month initial term followed by month-to-month is the stronger trust signal for a new firm. Payment is typically Net 15 or Net 30 with a deposit of about one month's fees, and annual escalators of roughly **5–8% should be capped** ([Topify](https://topify.ai/blog/seo-agency-contract-terms-explained)). The contract needs an explicit **no-ranking-guarantee clause**. Standard templates state that rankings fluctuate and the provider is not liable for dropped listings ([PandaDoc](https://www.pandadoc.com/seo-agreement-template/)), and Google itself calls a promise of #1 rankings a red flag ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-advice-on-how-to-hire-an-seo/351710/)). Clients should own content through work-made-for-hire language, own every account and every link built, and receive their **AI-visibility tracking history** (prompts, baselines, competitor benchmarks) on exit. The firm keeps its own tools and methodology. Liability should be capped at **6–12 months of fees**, with the firm's negligence and breaches of law outside the cap. Responsibility for a risky tactic should sit with whichever party chose it ([Topify](https://topify.ai/blog/seo-agency-contract-terms-explained)). Confidentiality should be mutual. Client duties should include flagging site changes to files, URLs, analytics code and robots.txt in advance ([PandaDoc](https://www.pandadoc.com/seo-agreement-template/)). Where the firm touches analytics or CRM personal data, it is likely a **processor**. It needs a data processing agreement covering documented instructions, security, sub-processor approval, breach support, deletion on exit and audit rights, and analytics tools and freelancers must be mapped as sub-processors ([Sprintlaw UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/data-processing-agreements-for-uk-marketing-agencies/)). US CCPA service-provider terms and state auto-renewal laws were not verified and need counsel. The contract should also include an AI-content disclosure policy, given Google's scaled-content enforcement.

The third gate is **kickoff and provisioning**. A roughly **60-minute kickoff** sets SMART goals, a roadmap, the communication cadence, a single point of contact, access grants, a baseline audit and an analysis of the 3–5 competitors the client names ([Surfer](https://surferseo.com/blog/onboarding-seo-clients)). It is followed by KPI checkpoints at **90 and 180 days** ([Topify](https://topify.ai/blog/seo-agency-contract-terms-explained)), timed just ahead of the month-6 churn spike. Productized tiers can automate much of this gate. The client creates an account, adds a domain, connects Search Console and GA4 via OAuth, and triggers an automated crawl. Search Atlas's help center suggests SaaS tools pair this self-serve setup with human onboarding calls ([Search Atlas Help Center](https://help.searchatlas.com/en/articles/11823178-search-atlas-master-faq)). No primary source gave a standard onboarding duration. The firm should set its own SLA and measure it. Basic insurance is cheap: median premiums for marketing consultants are about **$704 a year for E&O**, **$890 for cyber** and **$357 for general liability** ([Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/consulting-business-insurance/resources/cost/marketing-consultant-insurance)).

## Clients own every account; the firm is a named, least-privilege guest

The access model should be simple enough to print on the sales page. **The client owns every account. The firm is added under named individual identities at the lowest role that does the job. Passwords are never collected through forms or email.** Surfer suggests requesting login details through the onboarding questionnaire ([Surfer](https://surferseo.com/blog/onboarding-seo-clients)); that is common practice and should be avoided. Every major platform supports delegated access, and the defaults below come straight from platform documentation.

| Platform | Default role for the firm | Key constraints |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search Console | Full user; Owner only for migrations, then remove | On offboarding, remove verification tokens as well as users, because a removed owner with a remaining token can re-verify ([Google](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7687615?hl=en)) |
| GA4 | Editor for tracking work, Marketer for conversions, Analyst/Viewer for reporting | Effective permission is the most permissive across account and property levels ([Google](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9305587?authuser=0)) |
| Google Business Profile | Manager | Each user needs their own Google Account; a 7-day lock applies after a role is granted ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100?hl=en)) |
| Google Tag Manager | Edit or Approve; client keeps Publish | No owner role; keep multiple admins ([Simo Ahava](https://www.simoahava.com/gtm-tips/user-permissions/)) |
| Google Ads | Standard via manager (MCC) link, **without ownership** | An owning manager account can edit the client's users and links ([Google](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9978556?hl=en)) |
| Shopify | Collaborator account with trimmed permissions | Two-step authentication required; expires after 90 days without login; reject unexpected requests ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/manual/your-account/staff-accounts/collaborator-accounts)) |
| WordPress | Named Editor or custom role; Administrator only when needed, one per person | Application passwords inherit the user's capabilities; require 2FA; audit quarterly ([eSEOspace](https://eseospace.com/blog/wordpress-user-roles/)) |
| Webflow | Agency/Freelancer guest | Firm needs an Agency/Freelancer Workspace plan; guest teams capped at 5 or 10 ([Webflow](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961349456915)) |
| Wix | Website manager | Cannot see billing or delete or transfer the site ([Wix](https://www.wix.com/seo/learn/resource/website-permissions-for-brands-and-marketing-agencies)) |
| GitHub (custom stacks) | GitHub App with minimal permissions on selected repos | Apps start with no permissions; each installing account must approve permission changes ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/creating-github-apps/registering-a-github-app/choosing-permissions-for-a-github-app)) |

For changing client sites, there are five methods, each with a distinct risk profile. **Read-only crawling** carries zero write risk and should be the default for audit and grader tiers. **Native CMS roles** keep changes in the client's source of truth with built-in revision history, but they are slower. A **branch-and-pull-request workflow** suits headless and custom stacks: the client's developers review and merge, and rollback is a revert. **Edge workers** such as Cloudflare Workers modify responses on the CDN, so crawlers receive final HTML. They are cheap, starting on a free plan, and they work around slow developer queues. But they require high-privilege DNS or CDN access and can conflict with the codebase if developers are not involved ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/edge-seo)). **JavaScript overlays** are the fastest. They create lock-in because changes vanish when the script is removed, they add page-speed overhead, and they can create "discrepancies between what crawlers see and what's in your source code" ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/reviews/alli-ai)). Several unverified sources suggest many AI crawlers do not execute JavaScript, which would make overlays invisible to the very engines GEO targets. That claim should be tested before the technical design commits to any overlay.

Change management matters because the downside is severe. In one documented CMS migration done without SEO input, a 302 redirect in place of a 301, a robots.txt that blocked resources and a broken sitemap cost **32% of organic sessions and 25% of branded clicks**, and recovery took a **nine-month engagement** ([Workshop Digital](https://www.workshopdigital.com/case-studies/recovering-search-rankings-after-cms-migration/)). The minimum controls are a staging or preview step for anything touching templates, robots.txt, redirects or canonicals; a written change log; client approval gates; pre- and post-deployment crawl diffs; and a rollback plan for each deployment. Remote-loaded code should be banned outright. In a documented sabotage case, a former supplier's theme fetched a remote file that injected a `noindex` tag once the site left the supplier's hosting, and the client lost its top rankings ([Search Engine Watch](https://searchenginewatch.com/?p=1877)).

Security risk concentrates in the firm's own credentials. A single compromised manager-account login "can expose every linked client account," and phishing that impersonates Google policy notices is the most common entry point ([SaaS Hero](https://www.saashero.net/google-ppc/google-ads-agency-account-takeover/)). The same vendor reports a phishing kit that registers attacker passkeys, which survive password resets. That claim comes from a single vendor blog and is unverified. Hostage-taking is the mirror-image failure. In a 2025 New Zealand case, an incumbent agency removed the client's own access to its ad account within minutes of hearing a new consultant was coming, and claimed the work as its property ([Sam Frost](https://samfrost.co.nz/blog/are-your-digital-ad-accounts-at-risk-of-being-held-hostage)). The firm should make the reverse a published policy: the client creates and pays for every account, and access is handed back within a stated number of business days. Day-to-day hygiene means MFA everywhere and a password manager with per-client collections and event logs (Bitwarden pitches SSO/SCIM deprovisioning and "who accessed what" logs to agencies, per [Bitwarden](https://bitwarden.com/en-gb/solutions/marketing-agencies/)), expiring share links for the rare shared credential, quarterly access reviews, and API keys and GitHub App private keys kept in a secrets manager. Mid-market buyers will send security questionnaires. Even Semrush's public security page leads with PCI DSS, encryption, pen tests and DPAs rather than SOC 2 ([Semrush](https://es.semrush.com/company/security)), so SOC 2 can likely wait until deal sizes demand it. No survey data confirms this.

## Borrow referrals through partners until your own exist

Every agency survey found says the same thing: **referrals win**. AgencyAnalytics' 2026 survey lists **referrals (85%) and word of mouth (72%)** far ahead of SEO and content (38%), outbound (33%), digital ads (26%) and social (19%). A new source, **AI-driven search results**, already appears at **11%** ([AgencyAnalytics](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/agency-benchmarks-2026-ai-search)). Databox's agency survey ranked referrals first and SEO second for actually winning deals ([Databox](https://databox.com/agency-client-acquisition-strategies)). A new firm has no referral base, so it has to borrow one. SparkToro found **15% of agencies name partner referrals as their top source**, and speaking at events has climbed to fourth place ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/)). Web designers, developers and PPC-only agencies that don't do SEO are natural partners, and white-label fulfillment for them gives a software-assisted firm early volume. Outbound is a bridge, not an engine. Of the 59% of agencies that have tried it, **9% call it very effective, 58% moderately effective and 33% not effective**. **73% name LinkedIn** as their best social channel ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/)).

The sales math is encouraging once leads are qualified. SparkToro reports that **39% of agencies close 25–49% of qualified leads and 32% close 50–74%**, and that **55% close deals within 1–6 weeks** ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/digital-agency-new-business-still-a-concern-referrals-still-rule-for-lead-gen-and-are-sales-pipelines-finally-recovering/)). Outbound and partner work can therefore produce revenue within a quarter. Paid acquisition should wait. Cross-industry Google Ads cost per lead was about **$70** in 2025 ([Shopifreaks on WordStream/LocaliQ](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-ads-cpcs-rose-to-5-42-in-2025-with-87-of-industries-seeing-increases-but-conversion-rates-climbed-to-8-18-as-automation-improves/)), but **no verified 2025–26 CPC exists for "SEO services" or "SEO agency"** keywords, and that auction is crowded with agencies and lead-gen sites. Pull live figures from Keyword Planner and test only long-tail niche-plus-intent terms, such as "dental SEO company," with a capped budget once retainer gross margin is known.

Positioning should do the work that a missing track record cannot. Clutch lists **65,550 SEO firms**, with an average monthly cost of **$3,199** and most projects under $10,000 ([Clutch](https://clutch.co/seo-firms/pricing)). In a field that crowded, generalists disappear. Vertical specialists show what focus can do. Rankings.io reportedly built **$13M in revenue** serving personal-injury law firms ([The Blueprint](https://ai.theblueprint.training/chris-dreyer/)), and a dental marketing agency is listed for sale at **$165K MRR across 79 clients** ([Website Closers](https://websiteclosers.com/businesses/sba-pre-qualified-dental-marketing-agency-performance-driven-165k-mrr-2000-aov-sticky-client-base-79-active-clients-growing-yoy/113976)). Both figures come from titles and listings and are unaudited. The free-tool lead magnet has a long precedent: HubSpot built Website Grader to generate "buzz, organic/viral traffic, inbound links and leads," and it graded more than **one million URLs** ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/blog/bid/4834/Website-Grader-Evaluates-Over-1-Million-URLs)). A niche grader that scores a business's SEO, local presence and AI visibility is cheap for this founder to build. It also earns links, feeds the firm's own SEO and gives cold outreach a specific, useful opening.

The advertising rules are strict in this category, and early marketing must follow them. Google Ads prohibits "guaranteeing top placement in Google Ads or organic Google Search results," claiming that Google endorses an SEO service, and falsely claiming Google Partner status ([Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6086777?hl=en)). The FTC's rule on reviews and testimonials bans fake and AI-generated testimonials, sentiment-conditioned incentives and undisclosed insider reviews ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). Any discounted pilot clients who later write case studies must be disclosed. CAN-SPAM "makes no exception for business-to-business email." It requires a working opt-out honored within 10 business days, a physical postal address and honest subject lines, with penalties of up to **$53,088 per violating email** ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/can-spam-penalties)). LinkedIn reportedly caps connection requests at about **100 a week** and flags mass automation ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/blog/linkedin-connection-limits-guide/)); the figure is unofficial. Put together, the evidence suggests a sequence. Spend the first quarter on niche, offer and a few pilot clients from the founder's network and partners, plus compliant personalized outreach led by a free mini-audit. Ship the grader by month six. Build compounding inbound through niche content, LinkedIn, vertical trade-event speaking and a Clutch profile through month twelve. Turn on paid search only when unit economics are proven. This sequence is synthesized from general agency data; no study compares go-to-market sequences for new SEO firms.

## The fatal risks are overpromising, unmeasurable ROI and other people's spam

Most of the risks that sink SEO businesses are predictable, and several are self-inflicted. The table below pairs each risk with the design response the functional and technical documents should encode.

| Risk | Evidence | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| Shrinking clicks erode reported ROI | 58% CTR loss at position one under AI Overviews; 68% zero-click ([Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-ai-overviews-now-reduce-top-ranking-pages-clickthrough-rates-by-58-according-to-new-ahrefs-research-analyzing-300000-keywords/); [TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/reports-data/google-zero-click-searches-68-percent-2026)) | Report leads, citations and visibility share alongside traffic; focus on transactional and local intent |
| Algorithm volatility | Core updates in December 2025, March 2026 and May 21–June 2, 2026; spam updates in June and September 2026 ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-may-2026-core-update-done-41435.html); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/)) | Update annotations in reports; contract language on fluctuation; diversify into paid and AI channels |
| Spam-policy exposure from automation | 837 of 49,345 monitored sites deindexed within days of the March 2024 update; link-scheme benefits "permanently lost" after neutralization ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/); [GEL Studios](https://www.gelstudios.co.uk/insights/may-june-google-core-update-26/)) | No mass AI pages, paid links, expired-domain or parasite tactics; human review gates in the content workflow |
| Cross-contamination of SEO and ads | Since December 2024, Google Ads disapproves ads pointing to pages removed by a manual action ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/)) | Risky SEO tactics now endanger the paid channel too; one more reason for a strict tactic policy |
| Review and testimonial violations | FTC rule penalties up to $53,088 per violation; first warning letters to about a dozen companies in December 2025 ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-3594404/)) | Review requests sent to all customers, not filtered by sentiment; no incentives conditioned on rating; logged consent |
| GEO overclaiming | Evidence rests on one 2023 preprint; up to 90% of AI citations change over time ([Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/we-found-the-paper-that-launched-geo-heres-whats-actually-in-it/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/24/exclusive-as-ai-threatens-search-profound-raises-96-million-to-help-brands-stay-visible)) | Sell measurement and work performed, never placement; show volatility bands in reports |
| Early churn from expectations and opacity | Churn spikes at months 6, 12 and 24; secrecy drives early terminations ([SEOmonitor](https://www.seomonitor.com/learning-hub/the-complete-strategy-guide-to-reducing-churn-increasing-client-retention-for-seo-agencies); [DecisionMarketing](https://www.decisionmarketing.co.uk/news/agencies-pay-high-price-for-seo-smoke-and-mirrors)) | Expectation scoring at intake; live portal; 90/180-day reviews; paid search for early wins |
| Credential compromise | One manager-account breach exposes all linked clients ([SaaS Hero](https://www.saashero.net/google-ppc/google-ads-agency-account-takeover/)) | Named identities, MFA, vault with event logs, no ownership links, incident playbook |
| Commoditization by platforms | White-label AI hubs (Search Atlas) and Adobe-Semrush distribution | Compete on vertical depth, implementation and accountability, not on raw tooling |
| Vendor and API dependency | GBP API is gated; Semrush units expire; vendor pricing changes ([GBP FAQ](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/faq); [diyai.io](https://diyai.io/ai-tools/seo/seo-data-apis/)) | Abstract the data layer so vendors can be swapped; this risk was not researched in depth |

Two risks deserve a caveat. Antitrust is unlikely to rescue organic clicks. Judge Mehta's September 2025 remedies imposed only modest limits and no Chrome divestiture, and the DOJ and states appealed on February 3, 2026 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/antitrust/google-search-remedy-to-be-appealed-by-state-attorneys-general)). An AI-first Google results page is the right base case. And several items in the 2026 update timeline come from a single trade source: whether the August and September 2026 spam updates were separate, the reported May 2026 extension of spam policies to AI responses, and the October 2026 fact-check guidance. Verify these before writing them into client-facing materials.

## Conclusion

The founder's original framing treats SEO as a set of tasks. The evidence says the value has moved to two things that are not tasks: **verified visibility**, meaning presence in AI answers as well as rankings, measured across several engines and tied to leads; and **accountability**, meaning ownership, transparency and reversible changes. Both are software problems as much as marketing problems. For a technical founder, the defensible asset is not a better audit or a cheaper article. It is an operating system that makes every recommendation evidence-based, every change logged and every client the owner of its own data. That system also answers the industry's oldest trust problem, which is why transparency is a product feature here, not a slogan.

Some important unknowns remain and should shape the first months of operation more than any plan. There is no current pricing survey, no data on SMB willingness to pay for AI visibility, no verified CPC for SEO-agency keywords, no causal evidence for most GEO tactics, and no research yet on ads fee models. The firm should be designed to learn these quickly. Instrument the grader, test published prices with pilot clients, and track which services clients renew. Lock the architecture only where the evidence is solid: client-owned access, human-gated content, swappable data vendors and outcome-based reporting.

## Decisions the founder must make before the FDD and TDD

The decisions below determine scope, data model, integrations and compliance requirements for the Functional and Technical Design Documents. Each lists the default this research supports. Where the evidence is weak, the founder's judgment or a quick validation test should override it.

| # | Decision | Default supported by this research | What it determines in the FDD/TDD |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Target vertical(s) and geography** | One vertical with high customer value and local or transactional intent (e.g., dental, legal, home services); US first | Grader scoring rules, keyword taxonomies, CMS integrations to build first, compliance scope |
| 2 | **Business model mix** | Agency-led hybrid: productized entry, retainer core, software as internal leverage; self-serve SaaS deferred | Whether multi-tenant self-serve, billing and plan gating are in v1 |
| 3 | **v1 service catalog** | Audit, keyword/competitor, local, content (human-gated), AI visibility, technical implementation, reporting, review management; link building redesigned as earned links/PR | Modules, workflows and data entities to build |
| 4 | **Paid search in v1 or phase 2** | Strong fit; decide after researching fee models, Google Ads API access and Partner requirements | Google Ads API integration, MCC structure, combined attribution model |
| 5 | **Prices and packaging** | Published tiers roughly as in the pricing table, validated with pilots; capped annual escalator | Plan and entitlement model, quoting and invoicing features |
| 6 | **Contract terms** | 3-month minimum then month-to-month; no-guarantee; client owns accounts, content, links and tracking history; liability capped at 12 months of fees; mutual NDA; DPA exhibit; AI-content disclosure | Contract generation, e-signature, data-retention and export-on-exit features |
| 7 | **Recommend-only vs implement** | Implement for 2–3 platforms common in the vertical via native CMS roles or Git PRs; overlays/edge only as a logged stopgap | Write-access integrations, change log, approval gates, crawl diffs, rollback |
| 8 | **Build vs buy (data and fulfillment)** | Rent data (DataForSEO plus free Google APIs; Ahrefs/Semrush only if seats are already paid); resell citations (BrightLocal); build the workflow, portal and AI-visibility layer | Vendor abstraction layer, cost model per client, API quota handling (e.g., URL Inspection 2,000/day/site) |
| 9 | **AI engines and metrics for visibility reporting** | Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity; report citation share, leads and conversions alongside traffic | Prompt-set design, sampling frequency, KPI definitions, report templates |
| 10 | **AI content policy** | AI drafts allowed; mandatory expert review and logged fact-check; no scaled page generation | Content workflow states, reviewer roles, audit trail |
| 11 | **Onboarding mode per tier** | Self-serve OAuth onboarding for audit/local tiers; human kickoff for retainers; target SLA set by founder | Intake forms, OAuth scopes, access-provisioning tracker, onboarding SLAs |
| 12 | **Security and data posture** | Named identities, MFA, password vault with event logs, secrets manager, DPA/processor stance, E&O and cyber insurance; SOC 2 deferred until deal size requires | Identity and permission model, logging, data residency, incident playbook |
| 13 | **Acquisition plan and budget** | Partners + compliant outbound + niche grader first; paid search capped until margin is known | Grader feature set, CRM/lead tracking, consent and CAN-SPAM handling |
| 14 | **Team and fulfillment model** | Founder plus contractors (expert writers, PR specialist) with a target client load per person | Role-based access, task assignment, capacity planning |
| 15 | **Markets and languages** | English/US only at launch; non-English markets unvalidated | Localization, GDPR/UK GDPR scope, hreflang tooling |

Some evidence should be collected before these decisions are final: live CPCs for niche SEO-agency keywords, Google Ads fee-model benchmarks, the status of the Google Business Profile API application, a direct check of Google's current spam-policy and AI-content documentation, a test of whether major AI crawlers render JavaScript, and pricing feedback from at least three prospective clients in the chosen vertical.
